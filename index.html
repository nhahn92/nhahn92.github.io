Introduction

During my two and a half years in the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have built a strong technical foundation across software engineering, algorithms, databases, operating systems, networking, and secure coding. Developing this ePortfolio has allowed me to bring all of that experience together and demonstrate the skills I will carry into my first IT role and later into a cybersecurity position as I continue advancing my training and experience.

Over time, I learned how interconnected technology fields truly are. CS‑465: Full Stack Development I, CS‑340: Client/Server Development, and CS‑360: Mobile Architecture & Programming taught me how modern applications are built, deployed, and maintained across multiple layers. CS‑300: DSA: Analysis and Design strengthened my ability to design efficient algorithms and evaluate trade‑offs in computing solutions. CS‑305: Software Security and CYB‑200: Cybersecurity Foundations helped me understand how vulnerabilities emerge in real systems and how secure coding, validation, and architectural decisions protect data and users. These experiences have only solidified my career plan: begin in IT, build a strong operational foundation, and transition into cybersecurity where I can apply both my technical and analytical skills.

Collaboration and Team Environments

Throughout the program, I worked in collaborative environments that mirrored real development teams and stakeholder interactions. In CS‑255: System Analysis & Design, I participated in requirement‑gathering exercises, team discussions, and design reviews that taught me how to communicate technical decisions clearly and respectfully to diverse audiences. In CS‑250: Software Development Lifecycle, I learned how collaborative planning, documentation, and iterative feedback contribute to successful software projects. I feel confident that I can work effectively with others.

Professional Communication

Clear communication is one of my strongest skills. In CS‑320: Software Testing, I wrote detailed test plans and defect reports that required precision and clarity. In CS‑499: Computer Science Capstone, I used industry‑standard code commenting to document API behavior, authentication flows, and architectural decisions so other developers could understand and extend the system. Courses like these helped me learn how to communicate technical concepts to both technical and non‑technical audiences, which is critical because misunderstandings can lead to real risks.

Algorithms and Computing Solutions

Algorithmic thinking has been essential throughout my coursework. In CS‑300: DSA: Analysis and Design, I evaluated time complexity, designed efficient solutions, and learned how algorithmic decisions impact performance. In CS‑340: Client/Server Development, I applied these principles to real applications by designing predictable data flows and optimizing server‑side logic. I have learned how to analyze problems systematically, evaluate trade‑offs, and implement solutions that scale.

Software Engineering and Tools

I gained extensive hands‑on experience with industry‑standard tools and frameworks. In CS‑360: Mobile Architecture & Programming, I built mobile applications using modern development practices. In CS‑230: Operating Platforms, I worked with operating system internals, process management, and system configuration. In IT‑140: Introduction to Scripting, I learned how automation and scripting support IT workflows. These courses taught me how to build, maintain, and modernize systems using real tools.

Security Mindset

Developing a security mindset has been one of the most important parts of my degree. In CS‑305: Software Security, I learned how vulnerabilities emerge through poor validation, unsafe data handling, and weak authentication flows. In CYB‑200: Cybersecurity Foundations, I studied threat models, risk analysis, and defensive strategies. In IT‑212: Introduction to Computer Networks, I learned how protocols, firewalls, and network segmentation protect systems. These experiences taught me to anticipate adversarial behavior, validate data explicitly, and design systems that minimize attack surfaces.

Capstone Enhancements

The artifacts in my ePortfolio allowed me to combine all of these skills into a single, cohesive project, the Travlr Getaways full‑stack application. I enhanced this application across three categories: Software Design and Engineering, Algorithms and Data Structures, and Databases. For Software Design and Engineering, I modernized the Angular admin application, implemented role‑based access control (RBAC), clarified authentication flows, and improved documentation across the entire codebase. For Algorithms and Data Structures, I redesigned search, sorting, and pagination logic to be more predictable and scalable. For Databases, I strengthened schema validation, added sanitization, implemented aggregation pipelines, and improved error handling and logging.

Career Readiness

Completing this ePortfolio has helped me recognize how my coursework, projects, and enhancements fit together to form a strong professional foundation. I am ready to enter the IT field with confidence, knowing I can troubleshoot systems, communicate clearly, collaborate effectively, and apply secure coding principles. As I continue earning certifications like CompTIA A+, Network+, and Security+, I will build on this foundation and transition into cybersecurity roles where I can apply my security mindset, analytical skills, and technical experience. This portfolio represents my growth, my abilities, and my commitment to continuous learning in the field of computer science.
